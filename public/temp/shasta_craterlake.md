# Mt. Shasta → Crater Lake → Ashland
### 8-day road trip from San Francisco · Fri Sep 4 – Fri Sep 11, 2026

*A note on dates: you wrote "Friday Sep 3," but in 2026 Sep 3 is a Thursday and Sep 11 is the Friday. I've built this as **Fri 9/4 → Fri 9/11** (7 nights). If you can actually get out Thursday 9/3, see "If you gain a day" at the end.*

## READ THIS FIRST — five things that change the plan

**1. Crater Lake boat tours are cancelled for 2026. Witches Cauldron is off the table.**
The Cleetwood Cove Trail — the only legal access to the lake's shoreline — closed in summer 2026 for a three-year rehabilitation of the trail, the failed marina bulkhead, and the restrooms. That means **no boat tours, no Wizard Island landing, no swimming, no walking down to the water** for the 2026, 2027, and 2028 seasons, with the trail targeted to reopen in 2029. Everything else is fully open: Rim Drive, all rim-level hiking, Rim Village, both visitor centers, Crater Lake Lodge, and Mazama Campground. This is the single biggest change to your list, and there's no substitute — the "crater within a crater" is only reachable by boat.

**2. East Rim Drive is an active construction zone in 2026.**
The park cancelled its Ride the Rim event this year because of repairs and paving on East Rim Drive, the Cleetwood work, and improvements at North Junction Overlook. Several of your east-side targets — **Mt Scott, Phantom Ship Overlook, Pumice Castle, Cloudcap** — may be reachable from only one direction, or subject to pilot-car delays. I've anchored your rim day on the reliably-open west side and listed east-side stops as "if open." Check the park's Current Conditions page the morning you go and ask at the entrance station.

**3. You're traveling over Labor Day weekend.** Labor Day is **Monday Sep 7**, so Fri 9/4 – Mon 9/7 is the busiest camping weekend of the year in both states. Book the first four nights **today**. Midweek (Tue 9/8 onward) will feel like a different trip.

**4. Mossbrae Falls is still illegal, and it's genuinely dangerous.**
Good news first: in June 2026 Union Pacific signed a 75-year lease with the City of Dunsmuir clearing the way for a legal half-mile boardwalk from the Hedge Creek Falls trailhead, with a new 150-foot pedestrian bridge and a viewing platform at the base. But it's a ~$21M project expected to take about five years, and it hasn't broken ground. Until then the only route is trespassing along active track — two people have been struck by trains since 2011 and a woman drowned in the Sacramento River trying to reach it in spring 2025, and Union Pacific is now ticketing. **My recommendation: skip it.** Hedge Creek (walk behind the falls) plus the three McCloud River falls give you a better waterfall day with no legal or safety exposure. Your call — but go in informed.

**5. Fire season is the wildcard.**
Shasta-Trinity has 2026 fire restrictions in effect since July 1 (campfires only in Forest Service rings at designated developed sites). Umpqua National Forest escalated to **Stage 2 Public Use Restrictions on July 24** — assume no wood campfire at Toketee and confirm whether propane stoves are still permitted. There's also an active **Castle Fire** closure in the southern Umpqua (Tiller district, South Umpqua Falls area) in effect through late December; it's roughly 40 miles from Toketee and doesn't block your route, but smoke drifts. Check `inciweb.wildfire.gov` and `fire.airnow.gov` the week before you leave, and pack N95s.

**One piece of luck:** the new moon falls on **Sep 11**, so you'll have a thin waning crescent or no moon at all every night of this trip. Best possible conditions for the Hwy 97 pull-offs and the Crater Lake rim.

# DAY 2 — Saturday, Sep 5
### Mt. Shasta core day · ~60 mi of local driving

This is your one full day in California, so it's dense. Sunrise is around **6:40am**.

**5:15am — leave for Castle Lake.** ~25 min from town on a winding road (Castle Lake Rd off W. A. Barr Rd). It's in Shasta-Trinity National Forest, not a state park.

**5:45am — hike to Heart Lake for sunrise.** ~2 miles round trip, steep, about 40 minutes up. The trail was recently rerouted and improved, so it's much easier to follow than older trip reports suggest. Heart Lake sits in a granite bowl with Mt. Shasta filling the frame behind it — this is the shot, and it only works at sunrise. Headlamps, layers, and be at the top by 6:30.

**8:00am — back down to Castle Lake.** Swim (the water is warm and shallow near the shore), picnic breakfast at the tables, unwind. You've earned it.

**10:30am — Dunsmuir.** ~35 min. **Hedge Creek Falls** is a quarter-mile walk from a small trailhead park on Dunsmuir Ave — you walk into the basalt cave behind the falling water. Ten minutes of effort, genuinely magical. Continue to the river overlook past the falls if you want another 15 minutes.

**Lunch in Dunsmuir.** Cafe Maddalena (dinner only, weekends — call) or Yaks on the 5 for a big burger. Dunsmuir Brewery Works is the reliable option.

**1:00pm — Castle Crags State Park.** ~10 min south, Exit 724.
- **Vista Point** — drive most of the way up, then a short walk to a bench with the full granite spire panorama and Shasta behind you. Do this at minimum.
- **Crags Trail to Indian Springs** — 2.5 mi round trip, moderate, ends at a cold spring pouring out of the rock.
- The full **Castle Dome** push is 5.5 mi round trip with 2,200 ft of gain. Skip it today unless you're feeling strong; you were up at 5.

**4:00pm — Lake Siskiyou.** Check into camp. Then:
- **Kayak rental** at the marina (roughly $20/hr) — get out on the water in the late-afternoon light. The lake has a 10 mph limit so it's quiet.
- Walk the **blue suspension bridge** on the 7-mile lake loop trail.
- **Sunset from the swim beach** — Shasta reflecting in flat water. Sunset is ~7:35pm.

**Optional after dark:** if you have any energy left, drive Hwy 97 north of Weed (~40 min) and pull off anywhere for stargazing. Otherwise save it for tomorrow — it's on your route north.

# DAY 3 — Sunday, Sep 6
### Mt. Shasta → Volcanic Legacy Byway → Crater Lake · ~200 mi · 4 hrs driving + stops

**Morning option A — McCloud River Falls (my pick).** ~30 min east on Hwy 89. The riverside trail from Fowlers Camp links **Lower, Middle, and Upper Falls** — about 3.5 miles round trip to see all three, or drive the paved McCloud River Loop to the overlooks. Middle Falls is the showpiece. Lower Falls is the swimming hole. This is the waterfall day that replaces Mossbrae.

**Morning option B — Everitt Memorial Highway.** Drive to **Bunny Flat** (6,950 ft) and **Panther Meadows** (7,500 ft) for alpine meadow walks, spring water, and the mountain from underneath rather than across. 30 min up from town. Note some Mud/Ash Creek forest roads and parts of the Mount Shasta Wilderness have camping/campfire restrictions this year.

**Morning option C — Black Butte.** 5.4 mi round trip, ~1,845 ft gain, zero shade. Only if you start by 7am and love a stairmaster. I'd cut it.

**Fill your bottles** at the **Big Springs headwaters** in Mt. Shasta City Park before you go — the Sacramento River comes out of the ground there and the whole town drinks it. Local ritual.

**1:00pm — north on I-5 to Weed, then Hwy 97: the Volcanic Legacy Scenic Byway.** You were right not to skip this. Where I-5 forks at Hwy 97 outside Weed, the road opens into high sage country with Shasta looming to your left the entire way. Pull over constantly.

Stops along the way:
- **Living Memorial Sculpture Garden** — 10 min from Weed, free, quietly powerful.
- **Lake Shastina** pullouts for the reflection shot.
- **Pluto's Cave** — ~20 min north of Weed off Hwy 97 (County Rd A12 to a dirt spur). A short walk into a collapsed lava tube. Bring **headlamps and sturdy shoes** — the floor is loose rubble, and the "cave" is genuinely dark past the first chamber. Half an hour, worth the detour.

**Then decide on stargazing.** The Hwy 97 corridor between Weed and the Oregon line has the darkest skies of your whole trip, and with the moon nearly new you'll see the Milky Way rise toward the SSW over Shasta with your naked eye. **But** you also want to get to Crater Lake before the entrance station gets sleepy.

My honest read: **push through to Crater Lake tonight** and stargaze from the rim instead — it's 7,000 ft, 50+ miles from any city lights, and the Milky Way arcs directly over the caldera. You lose the Shasta foreground; you gain the caldera. If the Shasta foreground is the point for you, stop on 97 at dusk (~8pm), shoot for an hour, and accept a 10:30pm camp arrival.

**Route on:** Hwy 97 north to Klamath Falls (~2 hrs from Weed), then Hwy 97 north and Hwy 62 west to the **Annie Spring / South Entrance**. Klamath Falls is your last full-service town — **groceries, ice, gas, water**. Do it there. Total Mt. Shasta → Mazama Village is about 3 hours of pure driving.

**Entrance fee:** $30 per vehicle, good 7 days. If you'll hit other federal sites this year, the $80 America the Beautiful pass also covers the Umpqua Hot Springs trailhead later in the week.

**Evening at the rim:** if you arrive with light, drive up to **Rim Village** and walk out to the **Sinnott Memorial Overlook** — the easiest and best first look at the lake. Then back down to camp.

# DAY 4 — Monday, Sep 7 (Labor Day)
### Crater Lake rim day · 33-mile loop + hiking

**First: get current conditions.** Stop at the **Steel Visitor Center** (park HQ, 3 mi below Rim Village) when it opens, or ask at the entrance station. Ask specifically: *is the full Rim Drive loop open, and can I reach the Mt Scott trailhead?* Construction changes week to week and this determines your day.

**Drive the rim counter-clockwise** (west rim first) so the lake stays on your right and pulling into overlooks is easy.

### The reliable west side
- **Discovery Point** — short walk from a pull-off to where prospectors first saw the lake in 1853.
- **Watchman Overlook** — the classic Wizard Island composition.
- **★ The Watchman Peak Trail** — 1.6 mi round trip, ~420 ft gain, to a historic fire lookout. This is the best hour you'll spend in the park: the whole caldera below you, Wizard Island dead center, and on a clear day **Mt. Shasta on the southern horizon**, 100 miles away — which is a lovely way to close the loop on your own trip. Do this in the morning while the air is clearest.
- **★ Garfield Peak** — 3.4 mi round trip, ~1,000 ft gain, straight up from Rim Village to 8,054 ft. This is your reliable substitute if Mt Scott is unreachable. Steeper and more exposed than the Watchman, and arguably a bigger payoff.
- **North Junction Overlook** — check status, improvement work underway in 2026.

### The east side — call ahead
- **Mt Scott** — 5 mi round trip, ~1,250 ft, to the park's high point at 8,929 ft. Your list had this and it's the best big hike in the park. Verify access first.
- **Cloudcap Overlook** — highest paved point on Rim Drive.
- **Phantom Ship Overlook** and **Pumice Castle Overlook** — the quieter east-side pullouts.
- **Plaikni Falls** — 2 mi round trip, easy, through old-growth to a spring-fed cascade. May be reachable from the north only this year.
- **Vidae Falls** — roadside cascade on the southeast rim near the Pinnacles turnoff, with picnic spots. Closest to park HQ, so most likely to be reachable regardless.
- **Sun Notch** — 0.8 mi easy loop, best Phantom Ship viewpoint.

### Views of Llao Rock
Best from the **north-west rim between Watchman Overlook and the North Entrance junction**, and from the Watchman summit looking north.

**Evening:** dinner at **Annie Creek Restaurant** in Mazama Village (or cook at camp), then **drive back up to the rim after dark** for stargazing. With the moon nearly gone this will be extraordinary. Bring every layer you own — it drops into the 30s–40s at 7,000 ft in September.

# DAY 5 — Tuesday, Sep 8
### Crater Lake → Umpqua National Forest · ~60 mi · 1.5 hrs

Crowds evaporate after Labor Day. This is the sweet spot of your trip.

**Morning:** anything you missed on the rim, or a slow breakfast. If you skipped Garfield Peak yesterday, do it now in the cool.

**Route:** North Entrance Road out of the park to **Hwy 138 west** — the Rogue-Umpqua "Highway of Waterfalls." About 1h15 to Toketee. Stops on the way:
- **Diamond Lake** — worth a 20-minute detour to the north shore for the Mt. Thielsen view across the water. Boat rentals at Diamond Lake Resort.
- **Whitehorse Falls** — 100-yard walk, roadside picnic area.
- **★ Watson Falls** — 0.8 mi round trip, moderate, to the base of a **272-foot** cascade, the tallest in southern Oregon. Don't skip this; it's a five-minute detour from Toketee.

**Afternoon — ★ Toketee Falls.** 0.8 mi round trip on a boardwalk-and-stairs trail to a viewing platform. Two tiers pouring out of a wall of columnar basalt. It's the most photographed waterfall in Oregon for good reason.

**Set up camp at Toketee**, then:

**★ Umpqua Hot Springs.** 15 minutes from the campground: Hwy 138 to FR 34 at milepost 59, cross the concrete bridge, 2.2 mi to FR 3401, then 0.7 mi to the lot on the left. The road is rough — take it slow.
- **0.6 mi round trip**, steep and often slippery. Cross the footbridge, right 0.1 mi, right again at the junction, climb 0.2 mi, then uphill left at the fork.
- Terraced travertine pools on a bare rock face **150 feet above the North Umpqua River**, water at 100–115°F, hottest pool at the top under an open-air shelter. Looks across at Surprise Falls.
- **Day use only, sunrise to sunset — strictly enforced.** No camping, no overnight parking, fines up to $5,000. Being in the area after sundown is itself prohibited.
- **$5 per vehicle, cashless** — pay via the Recreation.gov app, or display a Northwest Forest Pass or America the Beautiful pass. Federal sites here no longer take cash or check.
- Parking is about **10 cars**. It fills.
- **It's clothing-optional and it's busy.** Expect nudity. Not a problem for most couples, but worth knowing before you're standing there.
- Bring: water shoes, quick-dry towel, dry bag, a warm layer to change into, a headlamp, and a trash bag. Dark swimsuit if you wear one — the minerals stain light fabric.

**Timing tip:** afternoon is the crowded window. Consider a quick look today and come back at **sunrise tomorrow** — you may have it to yourselves.

**Also nearby if you want more:** the **North Umpqua Trail** runs 79 miles along the river with a trailhead right at Toketee — walk any pleasant chunk of it. **Fish Lake** is a rough forest-road detour and probably not worth the time; **Table Rock** near Medford is better done as a short hike from Ashland if you want it.

# DAY 7 — Thursday, Sep 10
### Upper Klamath whitewater · full day

**Meet ~8:00am, back to the Rogue Valley around 5:00pm.** Outfitters pick up in Ashland; the put-in is about an hour out.

**The rivers are different now.** Four dams came off the Klamath in 2024 — the largest dam removal in history — so flows are no longer regulated by hydropower releases and now rise and fall with rainfall. Two consequences for you:

1. **Book immediately and ask directly about early-September flows.** Some outfitters wrap their Upper Klamath season by mid-August; others run into fall. This is the question to lead with when you call.
2. **A new section opened up.** The old Copco reservoir drained to reveal **Wards / "Kikacéki" Canyon**, underwater since the early 1900s and ancestral Shasta Nation homeland. It's now run as a **half-day trip packed with Class III–IV+ whitewater** through basalt walls nobody had seen in a century. If it's running, this is the most interesting rafting in the region right now.

**Outfitters, all Ashland-based:**
- **Momentum River Expeditions** — 3195 E Main St #2, Ashland. Small, guide-owned, the Upper K is their home river. Hotel pickup in Ashland. Half-day through multi-day.
- **Noah's River Adventures** — 53 N Main St, downtown Ashland. Runs both **Hell's Corner** (full day, Class IV) and **Wards Canyon** (half day, Class III–IV+), plus multiple pickup points.
- **Indigo Creek Outfitters** — meets in Phoenix, OR (10 min from Ashland), 8am–5pm full day on Hell's Corner.

**If Upper Klamath flows are too low**, the **Rogue River** is the fallback — it's dam-released from Lost Creek Lake so it runs reliably all summer. Half-day and mid-day Class III–IV trips depart daily from Ashland. Less dramatic canyon, guaranteed water.

**Evening:** you'll be tired and sunburnt. Ashland Creek Bar & Grill, Caldera Brewing, or Standing Stone. Second OSF show if you're inspired.

### Night 2 · Lake Siskiyou Camp Resort
4239 W. A. Barr Rd, Mt. Shasta · **530-926-2618** / 888-926-2618 · lakesiskiyouresort.com
Exactly what you described: panoramic Shasta views across the water, tent sites, RV sites, retro trailers, cabins and houses, a marina renting kayaks/canoes/pedal boats/patio boats, a 600-ft swim beach, a deli and taco shop, and the lake loop trail with the blue suspension bridge. Cabins are self-contained with kitchens; **26 rental units total**, so Labor Day Saturday will be gone fast. They book by phone, email, and through Campspot/Hipcamp.

*Candor: reviews are mixed. The setting is unanimously loved; cleanliness and customer service draw complaints, especially on the cabins. If you want a guaranteed-clean night, tent-camp here for the view and put your money into a nicer motel elsewhere.*

**Backups nearby:**
- **Castle Crags State Park** — Riverside and main campgrounds, 12+ sites, ~$15, flush toilets, showers, bear lockers, suspension bridge to the river. **Currently first-come, first-served with self-registration** (checked 8/12/26) — so it's a genuine backup, but arrive early. Right next to I-5, so expect highway and train noise.
- **Fowlers Camp** — 39 sites on the McCloud River at 3,300 ft, piped water, vault toilets, trail to all three McCloud Falls right from camp. **31 sites reservable on Recreation.gov, 8 first-come.** If you'd rather wake up at the waterfalls than the lake, swap Night 2 here.
- **Panther Meadows** — 15 walk-in tent sites at 7,500 ft on Shasta itself, free, first-come, no water, 3-day annual limit. Sublime and spartan. Cold in September.
- **McBride Springs** — 12 sites at 5,000 ft on Everitt Memorial Hwy, filtered Shasta views.
- **Jubilee Railroad Wilderness Lodge** (formerly Railroad Park) — caboose lodging and vintage cabins at the base of Castle Crags, plus RV/tent sites with hot showers. Novelty value, close to Dunsmuir.
- **Mount Shasta KOA** — 3,500 ft, cabins and deluxe cabins, open year-round.

⚠️ California requires a **free campfire permit** year-round for any campfire *or camp stove* on Shasta-Trinity land — get it at `permit.preventwildfiresca.org` before you go.

### Nights 3–4 · Mazama Village Campground, Crater Lake NP
**Reservations required July 1 – Oct 1 via Recreation.gov** (campground 10337002). 214 sites, 6 miles south of Rim Village just inside the Annie Spring entrance, at 6,000 ft in old-growth lodgepole. Loops A–G, mostly tent/car camping.
- Tent site $35–38 · RV no hookups $35–38 · electric $48–50 · full hookups $57–59
- Every site: picnic table, fire ring with grill, **bear-resistant food locker**
- Flush toilets, potable water, coin showers (Loop F and the camper store), laundry, dump station, camp store with groceries/firewood/gas, EV charger
- **Annie Creek Restaurant** across from the store
- Cell service is limited to nonexistent. Download maps before you arrive.
- Note: you reserve a *site type*, not a specific site — you pick from what's open on arrival.
- Sept 30 is the last night of the 2026 season, so you're near the end of the window.

**Labor Day Sunday will likely show sold out.** Two real workarounds: (1) set a Recreation.gov alert — cancellations are constant, and campers report the "campground full" sign being wrong; (2) book one of the alternatives below and move if something frees up.

**Alternatives near Crater Lake** (the park has no other campground, and nearby lodging is thin — you were right about this):
- **The Cabins at Mazama Village** — 40 rooms in 10 fourplexes right next to the campground, two queens, private bath, ~$200/night, no phones/TV/AC, no cooking, no pets. Book at explorecraterlake.com or **541-314-9777**. These sell out but do get cancellations.
- **Crater Lake Lodge** — 71 rooms on the caldera rim, open May 15–Oct 13, 2026. Books a year out. Worth checking for one splurge night. Online booking only, best rates direct.
- **Diamond Lake Resort** — ~45 min north via the North Entrance, cabins and motel rooms, boat rentals
- **Diamond Lake / Broken Arrow / Thielsen View campgrounds** — Recreation.gov, large and often available
- **Union Creek Resort** — historic cabins on Hwy 62, ~25 min west, next to Beckie's Cafe (get the pie)
- **Prospect Historic Hotel** — ~45 min west, three waterfalls within 2 miles
- **Fort Klamath** (~30 min south): Crater Lake Resort, Aspen Inn, Jo's Motel
- **Chemult** (~35 min NE on Hwy 97): Dawson House Lodge and basic motels — unlovely but available
- **Klamath Falls** — 60 miles south, full services, real hotels, your guaranteed fallback

### Night 5 · Toketee Lake Campground, Umpqua NF
**33 sites, ~$16/night, reservable on Recreation.gov (campground 251907) with a 7-day advance requirement**, plus a few first-come sites. Concessionaire-run. On the North Umpqua where it pools at Toketee Lake — willow, maple, alder, beaver and otter, and blue heron. 1.5 miles from Toketee Falls, 15 minutes from the hot springs.

- ⚠️ **Dry campground — no potable water.** Bring everything.
- Vault toilets. Limited trash service — pack it out.
- $5 extra vehicle fee. Quiet hours 10pm–6am.
- Mosquitoes are a known issue; the access road is short but bumpy.
- Campfires only in developed rings **when conditions allow** — and with Stage 2 restrictions currently in effect, assume no wood fire. Confirm the propane rule at 541-496-3532 (Diamond Lake RD, open Wed & Fri 8:00–4:30).

**Alternatives:**
- **★ Umpqua's Last Resort** — Dry Creek, 27 mi east of Idleyld Park. **Glamping tents, camper cabins, a tiny home**, 15 full-hookup RV sites, heated shower house, WiFi, river access. This is your "camping but with a real bed and a hot shower" option and it's a good midweek splurge. Private property, so it's outside the Forest Service fire restrictions.
- **Whitehorse Falls Campground** — 5 sites, $5–10, right on the byway, no water, no reservations
- **Boulder Flat / Horseshoe Bend / Poole Creek** — more North Umpqua options
- **Roseburg** — an hour west, full hotels if you want a bed and a laundromat

# LOGISTICS & PACKING

**Passes and permits**
- Crater Lake entrance: **$30/vehicle**, 7 days
- **America the Beautiful** annual pass ($80) covers Crater Lake *and* the Umpqua Hot Springs trailhead — worth it only if you'll visit other federal sites this year
- **Northwest Forest Pass** or $5/vehicle via the Recreation.gov app for Umpqua Hot Springs. **Cashless** — federal rec sites in Umpqua NF no longer accept cash or check.
- **California campfire permit** — free, required year-round even for camp stoves on Shasta-Trinity land: `permit.preventwildfiresca.org`

**Check these before you leave, and again mid-trip**
- `nps.gov/crla/planyourvisit/conditions.htm` — Rim Drive and trail status
- `inciweb.wildfire.gov` — active fires
- `fire.airnow.gov` — smoke and air quality
- `fs.usda.gov/r06/umpqua/alerts` and `fs.usda.gov/r05/shasta-trinity/alerts` — closures and fire stage

**Weather to expect**
- Valley floors (Redding, Dunsmuir, Ashland): 80s–low 90s daytime
- Mt. Shasta area: 70s–80s days, 45–55°F nights
- **Crater Lake rim at 6,000–7,000 ft: 60s–70s days, 30s–40s nights.** September snow is possible, if unlikely. Pack a real jacket, hat, and gloves. Most visitors underestimate this by 20 degrees.

**Pack list highlights**
- Headlamps (Heart Lake pre-dawn, Pluto's Cave, hot springs)
- Water shoes + quick-dry towels + dry bag (Castle Lake, Lower McCloud Falls, hot springs)
- Warm layer to change into immediately after soaking
- **Extra water containers** — Toketee has no potable water, and Panther Meadows/Algoma don't either
- N95s in case of smoke
- Offline maps for Crater Lake, Toketee, and the Hwy 97 corridor — cell service is unreliable to absent in all three
- Bear-aware habits: lockers at Mazama, Fowlers, and Castle Crags. Bears in the Castle Lake and Heart Lake drainages have gotten notably bolder about food.
- Binoculars — the caldera and the dark skies both reward them

# ON "PLANT MEDICINE"

I wasn't sure which of two things you meant, so briefly, both:

**If you meant the Mt. Shasta wellness scene:** the town is a long-standing hub for it. Herbal apothecaries and metaphysical shops line the downtown blocks, and **Stewart Mineral Springs** in Weed (mineral baths and a wood-fired sauna, ~20 min from Mt. Shasta City) and **Sacred Mountain Spa** in town are both easy to fold into Day 2 or 3 — a soak after Heart Lake would be well-timed.

**If you meant psychedelics:** Oregon has a state-licensed psilocybin services program (Measure 109, 2020), and **Ashland has become one of its main centers** — Satya Therapeutics, Omnia Group, Confluence Retreats, Aurora Healing Garden, and the Ashland Healing Center all operate there, with most clients coming from out of state. It's a supervised-services model rather than healthcare: no diagnosis or referral, must be 21+, with screening exclusions for active psychosis, current suicidal ideation, and recent lithium use. Sessions run roughly $900–$3,500 and involve a preparation meeting, a full-day dosing session, and integration — so it's a day you'd have to build the trip around, not slot into an afternoon. It requires booking well in advance, and it remains federally illegal. The official licensee directory is at oregon.gov (Oregon Health Authority → Oregon Psilocybin Services). **Nothing comparable is legal in California**, so Mt. Shasta isn't where this happens.
